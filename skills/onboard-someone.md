# Onboard someone

Get a new person from their first day to genuinely useful. The measure is not whether they had a
nice first week — it is how long before they do something that would have needed you, without
needing you.

## When to run this

Starts a fortnight before the start date, not on it. Runs for four weeks after.

## Why this is a skill and not a checklist

The laptop and the logins are a checklist and someone else should own them. The part that repays
writing down is the sequence of context: what a person has to understand, in what order, before the
next thing makes sense. Companies get this backwards constantly — tools in week one, strategy in
month three — and then wonder why new people make locally sensible, globally wrong decisions.

## Steps

1. **Before they start, write down what "useful" means for this person at 30 and 90 days.** One
   sentence each. If you cannot, you have hired against a feeling and the first month will drift.
2. **Send the operating manual and the direction files before day one,** and say plainly that
   nobody is expected to have absorbed them. The point is that they exist and are findable.
3. **Week one is direction, not tools.** Mission, north star, strategy, guardrails — what the
   company is for, and what it has decided not to do. A person who learns the tools first optimises
   the wrong thing competently.
4. **Give them one small real thing in week one.** Not a training exercise. Something that ships,
   with a short leash and a named person to ask. Confidence comes from a real result.
5. **Name the person they ask stupid questions.** Not their manager. A peer, explicitly told it is
   part of their job this month. Without a named person, new people guess and hide it.
6. **Week two, the rhythm.** Which meetings, what each is for, what is expected of them in each.
   Hand them `os/cadence.json` and walk it once.
7. **Week three, their number.** Run `set-accountability`. If they cannot have a number yet, say
   why and say when — an unexplained absence reads as the company not knowing what they are for.
8. **Week four, ask them what was confusing** and write the answer into `onboarding.md`. This is the
   only moment anyone can see the company with fresh eyes, and it is gone forever a fortnight
   later. A company that never updates its onboarding file is a company that stopped listening at
   the exact moment someone could see it clearly.

## Done when

The 30-day definition of useful is met or explicitly renegotiated, they have a documented number,
they know which meeting decides what, and `onboarding.md` carries at least one thing their
confusion taught you.

## Never

- Never start someone without a named first task and a named person to ask.
- Never let onboarding be a folder of links. A link is not an explanation and nobody reads twelve.
- Never skip the week-four debrief because they seem settled. Settled is when the fresh eyes close.
- Never give a new person a goal in week one to seem organised. A number they had no part in
  agreeing is a number they will not own.
