# Run a one-to-one

The recurring conversation between a person and their manager. Most companies run it weekly,
badly: a status update that both people could have read, followed by twenty minutes of nothing in
particular. The fix is not more preparation. It is two different meetings that ask different
questions, and knowing which one you are in.

## When to run this

Weekly, same slot, protected. Monthly, the same slot becomes the longer conversation instead.
Cancelling is a decision with a cost; treat it as one.

## What you need in front of you

- `os/accountability/goals.md` — their number and its target. The same number the chart shows.
- `data/commitments/` — what they said they would do, and what you said you would do.
- `os/people/expectations.md` — what good looks like at their level, for the monthly.

## Steps — the weekly

1. **Do not open with status.** If you need the numbers read to you, you have not read the weekly
   report and you are spending their time on your homework.
2. **Start with what is in their way.** The first question is theirs, not yours. Silence here is
   information: a person with nothing in their way every week is either unblocked or not telling
   you.
3. **Go to the number once.** Where it is, what they expect by the end of the quarter, what would
   change it. If the number is red and has been red for three weeks, that is a different
   conversation and you should say so out loud rather than working around it.
4. **Close the loop on last week's commitments,** both directions. Yours first — a manager who
   chases and never delivers teaches the meeting is theatre.
5. **Leave with at most three commitments,** each with a name and a date. Write them down where
   next week's run will read them.

## Steps — the monthly

6. **Change the question.** Not "how is the quarter", but "are you getting better, and at what".
   The weekly is about the work; the monthly is about the person doing it.
7. **Say where they stand against the level above.** Specifically, with an example from the last
   month. A person who is surprised at review time was failed here, not there.
8. **Ask what they want next,** and whether anything you are doing is in the way of it.
9. **Write the monthly down.** Not the pleasantries — the assessment. In twelve months this is the
   record of whether you told them the truth as you went.

## Done when

Commitments are recorded, the person could state their own number without looking, and nothing was
said in the meeting that will be a surprise in a performance review.

## Never

- Never use the weekly to deliver a performance judgment you have been saving. It belongs in the
  monthly, and it belonged there a month ago.
- Never let the meeting become a status report. If it has, the report is missing, not the meeting.
- Never take a commitment you do not intend to keep in order to end the conversation warmly.
- Never cancel two in a row. The second cancellation is the message, whatever you intended.
